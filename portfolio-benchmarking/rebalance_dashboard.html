What This Model Actually Solves
1

Objective — minimize tracking error, net of turnover

Minimum-tracking-error mean-variance optimization against the repo's existing policy benchmark (60% Mkt-RF / 25% RMW / 5% SMB / 5% HML / 5% CMA Fama-French factors -- a max-Sharpe long-only mix with a floor under every factor so none get zeroed by a noisy sample mean, see construct_benchmark_mvo.py), plus a per-position L1 turnover penalty λ · Σi |xi − x0i| charged on each (ticker, account) row separately. No return forecast is used.

2

Active CVaR — computed, not a constraint

5th-percentile Active CVaR is still estimated for every path from overlapping 12-month windows over the common price history of all holdings, and shown for context (Risk Attribution tab, path cards) — but no path below is required to satisfy any floor on it. An earlier version of this model enforced a -15% CVaR floor; that constraint has been removed from Paths C, D, and SAA.

3

Constraint — annual tax budget

Only net sales in the Joint (taxable) account cost anything. Taxed at a blended 29.5% long-term rate and an assumed 40.8% ordinary+NIIT rate on any short-term portion. Capped at 2% of household value per year.

4

Constraint — minimum per-ticker allocation

Every currently-held ticker keeps at least 0.3% of household value, summed across accounts if held in both. A structural floor, not a tax or risk rule — its only job is to stop the optimizer from fully zeroing out a small sleeve as an incidental side-effect of chasing tracking error or the tax budget. Applies to every path below, EXCEPT tickers in no-add status (next step).

5

Constraint — no-add tickers (SHV)

SHV may be trimmed or sold, but never bought further, in every path below and in both optimizer scripts — a plain per-row upper bound (x ≤ x0) rather than a tax or risk rule. SHV's current combined weight (0.298%) sits just under the 0.3% allocation floor in step 4, so it is exempted from that floor entirely: requiring both "no more than its current weight" and "at least 0.3%" on the same ticker would be infeasible by construction.

6

Path C vs. Path D vs. Path SAA vs. Path SAA 3%

Path C and Path D solve the identical tax-budget-only problem (optimize_tax_cvar_mvo.py, no CVaR constraint): MSTR's realized tax is carved out of the 2% budget's accounting entirely (a separate taxable strategy for MSTR is assumed handled elsewhere), and each caps how much of MSTR's current position may be sold this year — 70% for C, 30% for D — modeling a faster or slower multi-year glide path instead of a one-shot exit. Path SAA is a fully independent optimizer (optimize_saa_mvo.py) targeting the all-equity sleeve policy instead of the FF5 benchmark, with its own 2% tax budget and a 50% MSTR sale cap, using the identical carve-out device. Path SAA 3% is the same SAA optimizer and objective, but with the tax budget raised to 3% AND the MSTR carve-out removed — MSTR's own realized tax now counts against that 3% budget like any other position, so a larger MSTR sale competes directly with every other trade for the same dollars, rather than being exempted from the constraint entirely. All four are solved as convex quadratic programs via scipy.optimize.minimize(SLSQP).

"Track. Error" vs. "TE vs. SAA Bench." on every path card above are deliberately two different numbers, not a rounding artifact. "Track. Error" is each path's own NATIVE benchmark — the FF5 factor max-Sharpe benchmark for Current/Path C/Path D, but a completely different 13-sleeve Excel policy target for Path SAA/SAA 3% — so that column is not comparable path to path: Path C's 9.00% and Path SAA's 4.46% are two different rulers, not two points on the same one. "TE vs. SAA Bench." re-scores every path's SAME already-optimized post-trade weights (no re-optimization, no trade changes) against the ONE SAA sleeve/policy benchmark, so that column IS directly comparable across every path — on it, Path C and Path SAA turn out to be much closer to each other than "Track. Error" implies.

7

Turnover penalty — λ = 0.02

Without a trading penalty, minimum-tracking-error MVO is indifferent between a small trim spread across the book and a wholesale liquidation into whichever holding hedges best — the classic corner-solution failure mode. The L1 penalty in step 1 prices that: it leaves genuinely worthwhile trades near full size while zeroing out the long tail of cosmetic few-hundred-dollar trades. |x − x0| is linearized with a two-sided slack per position, so the problem stays a convex QP. At λ = 0.02 this cuts the trade list by roughly a third and the realized tax bill along with it, for a few tens of basis points of tracking error. Note per-position size limits still aren't imposed beyond what was asked for — the penalty discourages concentration, it doesn't cap it.

8

Risk attribution — TE, CVaR and exposure z-scores by factor

The Risk Attribution tab (portfolio_risk_attribution.py) decomposes each path's headline numbers onto the 5 FF factors. Tracking error uses an exact Euler decomposition (TE = Σk ak(Σa)k / TE, which sums to TE exactly); CVaR is attributed by averaging each factor's realized return over the SAME worst-5% scenario tail used to compute it (informational, not a constraint on any path), leaving a residual for what the 5 factors don't explain (overwhelmingly MSTR's single-name risk). SAA's FF5-factor risk numbers are attribution of what its sleeve-covariance-optimized weights happen to expose the household to on the FF5 benchmark, not something its own objective targets. Exposure z-scores standardize each ticker's factor betas across this household's own 16-ticker holdings universe, not a broad market cross-section — they read as "tilted relative to what this family already owns," which is the right question for a reweighting-only mandate.

9

Idiosyncratic tracking error and asset-class rollups

The factor-only TE above is exactly what the optimizer's objective sees, and it's a real simplification: every ticker's FF5 regression leaves a residual, and for a position the size of MSTR that residual dominates, not rounds away. A structural ("Barra-style") split adds it back without touching the optimizer itself: each ticker's annualized OLS residual variance, times the SQUARE of its total household weight (summed across accounts — the benchmark holds no individual stocks, so a ticker's total weight IS its idiosyncratic exposure), summed across tickers under a no-cross-correlation assumption, gives Varidio; TEtotal = √(TEfactor² + Varidio). Because factor and idiosyncratic variance are orthogonal blocks of one quadratic form, Euler's theorem still applies exactly to the combined total — verified as an assertion in the script, same as the factor-only split. Per-ticker idiosyncratic contributions roll up to the fixed asset-class labels in ASSET_CLASS by plain summation, which is exact (not an approximation) under that same no-cross-correlation assumption. The Active-CVaR residual on the Risk Attribution tab is NOT further split this way — that would need each ticker's own idiosyncratic return summed over the same tail scenarios, not just its residual variance, a separate piece of work this pass didn't add.

10

Data used

Holdings, cost basis, and ST/LT unrealized gains are read directly from the brokerage export. Monthly price history for all tickers is real, pulled via yfinance. This dashboard itself is generated purely from optimize_tax_cvar_mvo.py's saved CSV/JSON outputs by generate_rebalance_dashboard.py — no numbers are computed here.
